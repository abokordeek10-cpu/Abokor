# Monopoly lens
Score: 3 | Vote: PASS

## What is strong
- The founders' own constraints (AI discloses itself, the owner approves every price, German server, no cold e-mail) suit a cautious, low-tech German trade owner. If kept, this could grow into a trust brand.
- Monthly service on top of setup could build switching costs. Once enquiries, quotes and follow-ups run through Abora, leaving means rebuilding the office routine.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No secret and no moat.** The stack (n8n + Claude + a form/e-mail connection) is public and can be copied by any agency, freelancer or trade-software vendor in weeks. Nothing here is ten times better. It is the same thing other agencies sell, possibly cheaper. What stops a copycat in month six? Today, nothing.
2. **Distribution does not exist yet.** One cold call (no interest), 0 TikTok views, 16–58 Instagram views. The way to reach buyers is not proven, and nobody knows what one customer costs to win or serve. A good demo without a channel is not a business.
3. **The market is defined too wide to dominate.** "All trades, all of Germany, six modules" means a small share of a huge crowded market, the opposite of what this lens wants. The platform vision expands before owning anything.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What do you know about trade-business office work that an agency or trade-software vendor does not? Name the secret in one sentence.
- Which one trade, in which region, could you be the obvious choice for? How many such businesses are in Rhein-Neckar? (Open: market size, /founder-competitors and /founder-consumer.)
- Who already sells this to the same owner: agencies, trade software vendors adding AI, freelancers? (Open: /founder-competitors.)
- What does one customer cost to win and to serve each month, including your own hours? (Open: /founder-cfo.)
- What gets better for customer 20 because customers 1–19 exist: shared trade-specific prompts, data, templates, referrals within a trade guild?
- Why will this still be yours in ten years, once trade software vendors add AI themselves?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
The vote is PASS as pitched. It would move to FUND IF:
- The business narrows to one trade in one region (for example, plumbing/heating in Rhein-Neckar) and aims to win a large share of that small market before adding trades or modules.
- It names a candidate moat and a first step towards it: trade-specific know-how built from real enquiries, or a referral channel through a trade association or guild.
- It shows a repeatable channel: 3 paying pilots by 31 January 2027 won through one channel, with the cost per customer recorded.
- The CFO's numbers (/founder-cfo) show 149 €/month covers the cost to serve with room left over.
